Signs of fake followers

No profile photo, random username with many numbers, zero posts, very high following count with few followers, and the same generic comment on many posts.

Signs of fake engagement

Many likes with no comments, repeated emoji-only comments, sudden follower spikes with flat likes, and likers from unrelated countries or empty accounts.

How FollowLens checks

It reviews likers and commenters of a public post and flags accounts with these signals, then summarizes the result for the post.

What to do

Remove obvious bots, avoid buying followers and focus on content that real people like. Buying followers hurts your engagement rate.

Accuracy

The result is an estimate. A real person can have no photo, so use the flags as a guide, not a verdict.

What not to do

Do not buy followers or likes to fix low numbers, because fake accounts lower your engagement rate. Do not rely on one signal alone: a real person can have no photo. Look for several signs together on the same account.

Fake, bot and inactive: the difference

A fake or bot account is created to look like a person, often to follow many accounts or leave spam. An inactive account belongs to a real person who stopped using Instagram. Both lower your engagement rate, but only bots are a risk for spam comments and low trust.

Why fake engagement hurts

Brands and agencies often check engagement quality before working with an account, so many fake likes can cost you deals. Fake followers also dilute your reach because they never respond. Growing slowly with real people is a safer path.

A 5-minute manual check

Open 10 random followers. For each one look at the profile photo, number of posts, bio, and the ratio between followers and following. If most of the sample looks empty or spammy, run a post analysis to see the pattern across likers and commenters. A small sample is a hint, not proof.
