How to choose an Instagram non-followers checker

The best checker is not necessarily the one making the biggest accuracy claim. Evaluate how a service obtains information, what credentials it asks for, how it explains incomplete results, and what happens to your data.

1. Check password and session safety first

A tool should not ask you to hand over your Instagram password, session cookie, recovery code or one-time authentication code. Treat requests for these credentials as a serious warning. Prefer workflows that clearly explain what information they use.

2. Understand the data source

Ask whether the service compares public information, information you provide, or an official export from your own account. These methods have different privacy and completeness trade-offs. A service should explain what it can access and what it cannot.

3. Look for honest accuracy language

Instagram data can be restricted, delayed or incomplete. Avoid absolute promises such as “100% accurate for every account” unless the service can substantiate exactly what that means. A useful report should identify scan time and incomplete-data conditions.

4. Check historical tracking claims

Historical unfollow tracking requires a prior snapshot or another reliable historical record. A first scan cannot prove who unfollowed you before tracking started.

5. Read the privacy and deletion information

Look for a clear explanation of what usernames, scan results, logs and payment details are processed; why they are processed; how long they are retained; and how to request deletion. If a policy is vague, ask the operator before submitting data.

6. Understand pricing before scanning

Check free limits, paid credits, renewal terms, refunds and whether a failed or incomplete scan consumes credits. Do not rely on a search snippet or old review for current pricing.
