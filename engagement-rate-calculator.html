Instagram engagement rate calculator

What is a good engagement rate

It depends on account size and niche. Smaller accounts usually have a higher rate than large ones. Compare yourself with similar accounts, not with huge creators.

How to get average likes and comments

Take your last 10 to 12 posts, add likes and comments, then divide by the number of posts. FollowLens can calculate these averages for a public account.

How to improve it

Post when your followers are active, use clear first lines, ask a question, reply to comments quickly and remove fake followers that never engage.

Why followers alone mislead

A big follower count with low likes means weak reach. Engagement rate shows how many followers actually respond.

Worked example

An account has 2,000 followers and its recent posts average 80 likes and 6 comments. Engagement rate = (80 + 6) / 2,000 x 100 = 4.3%. If followers double to 4,000 and likes stay the same, the rate falls to about 2.15%.

Engagement rate by followers versus by reach

The formula on this page divides likes and comments by followers, which anyone can calculate on a public account. If you have access to your own Instagram insights, you can also divide by reach, which shows how many of the people who saw a post responded. Use one method consistently so your numbers can be compared over time.

How to compare fairly

Use the same period and the same post type, for example the last 12 posts or the last 12 Reels. A single viral post can distort an average, so remove outliers or use the median instead of the mean. Compare your rate with accounts of a similar size in the same niche.

Track it over time

Write down your rate every week or month. A rising rate with steady followers means your content is working. A falling rate after a follower spike often points to fake or inactive followers.
